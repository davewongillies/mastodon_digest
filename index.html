when i was 12 years old, my sister and i bought a sega genesis. i begged and pleaded for a copy of Toejam & Earl for christmas that year, to the degree that my mom said if i brought it up one more time i’d never get it. 😅

she did buy it for me in the end, at the astronomical cost of $100 CAD (in 1991 dollars - a LOT of money back then)

my sister and i played it to death in co-op mode, and it became the game that we still talk about 30+ years later. i wouldn’t even realize it was my first roguelike until my late 20s.

ten years ago i stumbled upon this underwatched interview with Toejam & Earl co-designer greg johnson. and i see that it is still criminally underviewed.

https://youtube.com/watch?v=xiakIFY5gBw

greg does something i’ve never seen before: he asks the interviewer dozens of questions about what makes the game fun to play, what would make it more enjoyable, and how he thinks the game could be improved.

it inverts a traditional game designer interview into a thoughtful, fascinating insight into player-led design. even if you’re not into computer games at all, greg’s insightful questions are a model for creative thinking in conversation

Here is an observation about writing software that may be so general as to be incomprehensible.

When you go from having one of a thing to having two of a thing, it is easier and possibly even more readable to just duplicate the thing instead of creating a generalized system that can handle N things.

Once you get to having three of a thing, it is better in all ways to simply have a generalized system that can handle N things.

But you don't *do* this when N reaches 3. You wait until N reaches 4

The problem and fix for the FAA outage:

Digging through AI gobbledygook to come up with a coherent description of the problem, here’s my understanding:

The SMART tool rollout and an update to Chicago FAA FTI network forced a reboot across 20 year old Cisco 600 series routers and legacy Time Division Multiplexing (TDM) copper switches. As those routers attempted to reestablish routing tables the surge of reconnection traffic from the smart AI system that tries to pull in 22 data feeds at once limited processors and memory buffers causing them to output corrupted radar telemetry and pilot comms traffic. Because Chicago is central East-West hub, that routing instability rapidly cascaded across 22 regional air route centers.

This affected pings to determine if the system was up and running and pilot to tower communications over the FTI network.
